2Problem & Opportunity

AI adoption is accelerating across the enterprise, but the way initiatives are identified, evaluated, and funded has not kept pace. Today there is no single intake channel, no shared scoring model, and no portfolio dashboard. Each business unit tracks its own ideas in its own way, which creates several compounding problems:

  • Initiatives are siloed: two teams can pursue nearly identical use cases without knowing it, duplicating effort and vendor spend.
  • Prioritization is subjective: without a common rubric, steering committees weigh presentations and persuasion rather than comparable scores for feasibility, value, data readiness, and security posture.
  • There is no portfolio view: leadership cannot see how much is being invested in AI, where the bets sit on a value-vs-feasibility map, or which initiatives are stalled.
  • Governance is inconsistent: security, compliance, and data- readiness checks happen at different stages (or not at all), raising the risk of a late-stage block or a production incident.

Who is affected

  • Enterprise Architecture and Strategy (no consolidated view)
  • Steering committees (no apples-to-apples comparison)
  • Business-unit AI leads (no clear intake path)
  • Security and compliance teams (late visibility into AI risk)
  • Finance (cannot track or benchmark AI portfolio spend)
  • Domain Squads (no standardized way to propose and track work)
Cost of inaction (do-nothing baseline)
Leave the current state in place and the enterprise continues to allocate AI investment on an ad-hoc basis. Conservatively, 15% of portfolio spend goes to low-value or redundant initiatives ESTIMATE that a transparent prioritization process would have deprioritized. On a $2M annual AI portfolio ESTIMATE, that is roughly $300K a year in misallocated capital, plus the compounding cost of delayed high-value work, governance gaps, and duplicated vendor contracts. The problem grows as the number of AI use cases grows.

3Strategic Alignment

The initiative directly advances Digital Transformation & Operational Excellence, the sponsoring team's stated strategic pillar, and maps cleanly onto Veralto Enterprise System (VES) principles:

  • Customer-centricity: a standardized scoring rubric ensures every AI initiative is evaluated against real customer and business impact, not internal politics.
  • Continuous improvement / lean: the self-serve intake and automated scoring cut waste from the evaluation cycle, reducing time-to-decision and freeing committee bandwidth for higher-order review.
  • Quality and problem-solving: built-in checks for data readiness, security, and compliance catch issues early rather than after significant capital has been spent.
  • Standard work: the portal establishes a repeatable, transparent process for AI initiative lifecycle management, from intake through post-deployment review.

FinAI-Strat also accelerates the broader goal of scaling AI adoption responsibly by giving every business unit a clear, governed path from idea to production.

4Proposed Solution & Scope

Build an interactive, web-based AI portfolio management portal hosted on Azure. The portal provides a centralized governance framework with decentralized execution through a hub-and-spoke "Domain Squad" operating model.

Architecture

ComponentTechnologyPurpose
FrontendVanilla JavaScript, CSS, HTML (Static Web App)Lightweight, modular UI with no heavy framework overhead
IdentityAzure Entra ID (SSO)Enterprise authentication and role-based access
Data storeAzure Cosmos DBScalable, schema-flexible persistence for initiative records and scoring data
HostingAzure Static Web AppsLow-cost, globally distributed hosting with built-in CI/CD

Core capabilities

  • Self-serve initiative intake: business units submit AI use cases through a guided form that captures all evaluation dimensions upfront.
  • Automated scoring engine: standardized evaluation across technical feasibility, business value, data readiness, and security/compliance, producing a composite score and a quadrant placement.
  • Interactive Prioritization Matrix: a visual value-vs- feasibility scatter plot that categorizes initiatives as Strategic Investments, Quick Wins, Incremental Improvements, or Reconsider, making portfolio balance immediately obvious.
  • Domain Squad views: each squad sees its own initiative pipeline, scores, and status while the Hub sees the full cross-enterprise portfolio.
  • Self-healing state management: schema versioning and coordinate logic in the app layer automatically patch and resolve data conflicts, reducing maintenance burden.

Governance framework

Hub-and-Spoke model: the centralized Hub (Enterprise Architecture & Strategy) provides the tooling, standards, and portfolio oversight. Decentralized Domain Squads, led by business units, drive specific use-case development and execution within those standards.

In scope

  • Initiative intake portal with guided submission
  • Scoring engine (feasibility, value, data, security)
  • Prioritization Matrix (interactive scatter plot)
  • Portfolio dashboard with filtering and drill-down
  • Domain Squad pipeline views
  • Azure Entra ID SSO integration
  • Cosmos DB backend with schema versioning
  • Role-based access (Hub admin, Squad lead, Viewer)

Out of scope (future phases)

  • Automated ML model deployment or MLOps integration
  • Financial system or ERP integration
  • Vendor procurement workflow automation
  • Advanced analytics or predictive portfolio modeling
  • Mobile-native application

10Resource & Organizational Impact

  • People: 1-2 developers (vanilla JS / Azure); architecture sponsor for design decisions; Domain Squad champions for pilot and feedback; security/compliance reviewer for Entra ID and data model sign-off.
  • Process change: AI initiative proposals shift from ad-hoc emails and slide decks to a structured portal submission. Steering committees review pre-scored, ranked portfolios instead of raw pitches. Domain Squads gain a self-service pipeline view.
  • Change management: onboarding sessions for each Domain Squad; executive communications positioning the portal as an enabler (not a gate); quick-reference guides and in-app help.
  • Skills: minimal new technical skills for end users. Squad leads need a short walkthrough on submission and scoring interpretation. Hub admins need training on rubric configuration and portfolio management.
